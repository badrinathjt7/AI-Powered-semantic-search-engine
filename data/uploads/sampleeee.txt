Many common laptop problems can be resolved at home through simple preventive maintenance and basic software/hardware troubleshooting, potentially helping you avoid professional repair services. 
Software Troubleshooting Tricks
Restart the device: The most basic, yet effective, trick for resolving temporary glitches, slow performance, and unresponsive apps.
Manage startup programs: Many programs automatically run when you turn on your laptop, slowing it down. Open the Task Manager (Ctrl + Shift + Esc on Windows), go to the "Startup" tab, and disable unnecessary programs.
Keep software updated: Outdated operating systems, drivers, and applications can cause performance and security issues. Ensure automatic updates are enabled or check for them manually.
Run antivirus/malware scans: Use a reputable antivirus program (like Windows Defender or third-party software) to scan for and remove malicious software that could be slowing your system or compromising your data.
Clear temporary files and optimize storage: Clutter on your hard drive can degrade performance. Use built-in tools like Disk Cleanup on Windows to remove junk files. For traditional Hard Disk Drives (HDDs), use the defragmentation tool to optimize data organization (this is not necessary for Solid State Drives, or SSDs).
System file check: If your laptop is crashing frequently, run the System File Checker tool (sfc /scannow) in Command Prompt (as an administrator) to scan for and repair corrupted system files. 
Hardware and Maintenance Tricks
Keep it cool: Overheating is a major cause of hardware damage. Use your laptop on a hard, flat surface to ensure proper airflow and avoid placing it on soft surfaces like beds or couches.
Clean vents and fans: Dust buildup can block airflow. Use a can of compressed air to gently blow dust out of the vents and keyboard.
Check power connections: If your laptop won't turn on or charge, ensure the power cord is securely plugged into both the laptop and the wall outlet. Try a different outlet or inspect the charger cable for damage.
Reconnect external displays: If an external monitor isn't working, check the physical cable connections, use a different port, or press Windows + P to adjust display settings.
Perform a hard reset: If the laptop freezes or won't turn on, disconnect all power sources (including the battery if easily removable) and hold the power button for 30 seconds to drain any residual power. Reconnect everything and try turning it on again.
Upgrade RAM or storage: If your laptop consistently runs slow, a simple and effective DIY fix is often to add more RAM or replace an old HDD with a faster SSD. Refer to your laptop's manual for compatibility and instructions. 
Important Precautions
Back up your data: Before attempting any significant fixes, back up all important files to an external hard drive or cloud storage like Google Drive to prevent data loss.
Proceed with caution: For internal hardware fixes, take your time, never force components, and use the correct tools. Look up guides or videos specific to your laptop model online (e.g., on sites like iFixit) before you begin.
Know your limits: If you are not confident in your abilities or the problem is complex (e.g., a "Blue Screen of Death" with cryptic error codes, or severe physical damage), seek professional help to avoid causing further damage.
